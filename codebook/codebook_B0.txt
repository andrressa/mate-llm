CODEBOOK B(0) — AFTER ROUND 0


ENTRIES

1. Explicit grounding
Annotate a concept only when it is supported by an identifiable word, expression, or mathematical symbol in the problem statement.

2. Textual support
A concept may be included when it is explicitly expressed in the natural-language text.

3. Symbolic support
A concept may be included when it is explicitly represented by mathematical notation.

4. Preserve explicit distinctions
Preserve distinctions that are explicitly encoded in the notation.

5. No unstated properties
Do not infer mathematical properties that are not explicitly represented.

6. No contextual inference
Do not add concepts solely from contextual or background mathematical knowledge.

7. No solution-strategy inference
Do not add concepts that are required only by a possible solution strategy.

8. Normalized terminology
Use the normalized labels defined in the codebook.

9. Inclusion criteria
Apply the inclusion criteria associated with each normalized label.

10. Exclusion criteria
Apply the exclusion criteria associated with each normalized label.

11. Synonymous duplicates
Remove synonymous duplicate labels from the same annotation.

12. One normalized label per concept
Represent a recurring concept with a single normalized label.

13. Mathematical content only
Annotate mathematical concepts rather than characteristics of the question or its presentation.

14. Direct evidence
Each annotation must be traceable to explicit textual or symbolic evidence in the statement.

15. No post-hoc derivation
Do not add a concept only because it becomes visible after solving, simplifying, or transforming the problem.

16. Fixed resources within a round
Use the same version of the prompt and codebook for every problem processed in the same annotation round.

17. Human validation
Model-generated annotations are preliminary and must be human-validated before they are treated as reference annotations.

18. Revision only after validation
Revise the codebook only after the round has been completed and the disagreements have been human-validated.
