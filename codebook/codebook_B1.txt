CODEBOOK B(1) — AFTER ROUND 1


ENTRIES

1. Explicit grounding
Annotate a concept only when it is supported by an identifiable word, expression, or mathematical symbol in the problem statement.

2. Textual support
A concept may be included when it is explicitly expressed in the natural-language text.

3. Symbolic support
A concept may be included when it is explicitly represented by mathematical notation.

4. Preserve explicit distinctions
Preserve distinctions that are explicitly encoded in the notation.

5. No unstated properties
Do not infer mathematical properties that are not explicitly represented.

6. No contextual inference
Do not add concepts solely from contextual or background mathematical knowledge.

7. No solution-strategy inference
Do not add concepts that are required only by a possible solution strategy.

8. Normalized terminology
Use the normalized labels defined in the codebook.

9. Inclusion criteria
Apply the inclusion criteria associated with each normalized label.

10. Exclusion criteria
Apply the exclusion criteria associated with each normalized label.

11. Synonymous duplicates
Remove synonymous duplicate labels from the same annotation.

12. One normalized label per concept
Represent a recurring concept with a single normalized label.

13. Mathematical content only
Annotate mathematical concepts rather than characteristics of the question or its presentation.

14. Direct evidence
Each annotation must be traceable to explicit textual or symbolic evidence in the statement.

15. No post-hoc derivation
Do not add a concept only because it becomes visible after solving, simplifying, or transforming the problem.

16. Fixed resources within a round
Use the same version of the prompt and codebook for every problem processed in the same annotation round.

17. Human validation
Model-generated annotations are preliminary and must be human-validated before they are treated as reference annotations.

18. Revision only after validation
Revise the codebook only after the round has been completed and the disagreements have been human-validated.

19. Limit target normalization
Normalized labels: 'limit at infinity', 'limit at zero', or 'limit' as appropriate.
Patterns: x -> infinity, n -> infinity, x -> 0, or a generic x -> a.
Inclusion: Preserve an explicitly encoded target of approach. Use 'limit at infinity' when infinity is part of the limit notation and 'limit at zero' when zero is the explicit target.
Exclusion: Do not annotate 'infinity' as a separate concept when it occurs only as the target of a limit.
Round-1 evidence: Questions 2, 8, 15, and 18.

20. Indexed radical normalization
Normalized labels: 'square root', 'cube root', 'k-th root', and 'nth root'.
Patterns: sqrt(x), indexed radicals, and expressions such as sqrt[n]{x}.
Inclusion: Preserve the explicit radical index. For a numeric index, use the corresponding specific root label; for a symbolic index n, use 'nth root'.
Exclusion: Do not reduce every indexed radical to 'square root'.
Round-1 evidence: Questions 2 and 9.

21. Factorial
Normalized label: 'factorial'.
Patterns: n!, (2n)!, or the word 'factorial'.
Inclusion: Annotate only when factorial notation or terminology is explicit.
Exclusion: Do not infer factorial from a product that is merely equivalent to one.
Round-1 evidence: Question 2.

22. Initial-value problem
Normalized label: 'initial-value problem'.
Textual patterns: 'initial-value problem', 'initial value problem', or an explicitly stated differential equation together with initial conditions.
Inclusion: Annotate when the statement explicitly presents an IVP.
Exclusion: Do not infer an IVP from a differential equation without initial data.
Round-1 evidence: Questions 1 and 11.

23. Derivative order
Normalized labels: 'derivative' and 'second derivative'.
Patterns: f'(x), y', g'(0), y'', f''(x).
Inclusion: Preserve explicitly written derivative order.
Exclusion: Do not replace an explicitly written second derivative by the generic label 'derivative' when the distinction is relevant to the notation.
Round-1 evidence: Questions 1, 9, 11, 12, and 14.

24. Explicit named theorem or method
Normalized label: use the explicit theorem or method name, with synonymous names normalized.
Patterns: 'divergence theorem', 'Gauss' theorem'.
Inclusion: If a theorem or method is explicitly named in the problem statement, it is eligible for annotation.
Exclusion: Do not add methods that are merely possible solution strategies but are not mentioned.
Normalization: 'Gauss' theorem' and 'divergence theorem' are normalized to 'divergence theorem' in the vector-calculus context.
Round-1 evidence: Questions 5 and 6.

25. Integral-type specificity
Normalized labels include 'integral', 'definite integral', and 'surface integral' when explicitly represented.
Patterns: integral bounds, double-integral over a surface, and explicit wording such as 'surface integral'.
Inclusion: Preserve an integral type that is explicitly named or symbolically encoded.
Exclusion: Do not assign a more specific integral type when the statement does not support it.
Round-1 evidence: Questions 6, 17, and 18.

26. Algebraic structures
Normalized labels preserve explicitly named algebraic objects, including 'finite field', 'algebraic closure', 'field extension', 'group', 'subgroup', 'cyclic group', 'ideal', and 'principal ideal'.
Inclusion: Annotate an algebraic structure only when its name or standard notation explicitly identifies it.
Exclusion: Do not infer additional properties, classifications, or theorems about the structure.
Round-1 evidence: Questions 3, 7, and 16.

27. Operators, adjoints, and linear functionals
Normalized labels: 'operator', 'adjoint operator', 'linear functional', 'dual space', and 'norm' when explicitly supported.
Patterns: A*, X*, Y*, the word 'adjoint', 'linear functional', and 'norm'.
Inclusion: Preserve the distinction between an operator, its adjoint, and a functional when explicitly stated.
Exclusion: Do not infer boundedness, continuity, or other operator properties not stated.
Round-1 evidence: Questions 4 and 10.

28. Function terminology and explicit functional structure
Normalized labels include 'inverse function', 'functional equation', 'exponential function', and explicitly named trigonometric functions.
Inclusion: Use the specific function label when the statement names the function or displays its standard notation.
Exclusion: Do not infer a function class from a manipulation or a possible solution.
Round-1 evidence: Questions 12, 13, 14, 15, and 19.
